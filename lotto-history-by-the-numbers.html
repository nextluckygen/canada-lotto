44 years of Canadian lotto draws, by the numbers

Written by NextGen, British Columbia · Last updated October 2026

Our archive holds every Lotto 6/49 draw since the first one in June 1982 (4,462 draws up to October 24, 2026) and every Lotto Max draw since September 2009 (1,281 draws up to October 23, 2026). This article goes through what that record shows: the busiest and quietest numbers, the strangest draws, the longest droughts, and how all of it compares with pure chance. Everything here describes the past. None of it tells you anything about the next draw.

Lotto 6/49: from weekly to twice a week

The first Lotto 6/49 draw in the archive was on Saturday, June 12, 1982. For the rest of 1982 there were 29 draws, then 53 in 1983 and 52 in 1984: one a week, on Saturdays. The first Wednesday draw appears on September 11, 1985, which is why 1985 has 68 draws. Since 1986, every full year has had 104 or 105 draws. The number range has never changed: six numbers from 1 to 49, plus a bonus number. That makes 6/49 one of the longest unbroken lottery records in Canada, and a good one for testing ideas about "hot" and "cold" numbers.

The most and least drawn numbers

Across 4,462 draws, each main-number slot has been filled 26,772 times. If every number were equally likely, each would expect to appear about 546 times. Here's what happened:

A gap of more than 100 between 45 and 14 sounds like strong evidence that some numbers are favoured. It isn't. With 49 numbers each landing about 546 times, random variation alone typically pushes individual counts 20 to 25 above or below average, and with 49 numbers you'd expect a few to land 50 or more away. The usual way to test this is a chi-square goodness-of-fit test, which measures how far the whole set of counts strays from equal. For 6/49 the statistic is about 42.2 on 48 degrees of freedom. A fair draw would give a value that size or larger about 70% of the time. In plain terms, 44 years of 6/49 look exactly like a fair, random draw.

If certain numbers were really favoured, the same ones would lead in every period. Splitting the record by decade shows they don't:

This is what randomness looks like: every period has leaders, and they're different each time. A "hot numbers" chart built from one stretch of draws tells you about that stretch only. Odd draws and record draws

The lowest and highest totals

Add up the six main numbers of a 6/49 draw and the average over the whole record is 151.65, very close to the theoretical average of 150. The extremes:

  • Lowest total, 45: October 29, 1983, when the draw was 1, 5, 7, 8, 11, 13. All six numbers came from the bottom quarter of the range.
  • Highest total, 263: October 21, 2000, when the draw was 38, 43, 44, 45, 46, 47. That's five consecutive numbers from 43 to 47.

Draws like these get noticed because they look like patterns. But 1-5-7-8-11-13 was exactly as likely as any other combination: 1 in 13,983,816.

Has a combination ever repeated?

No. In 4,462 Lotto 6/49 draws, no set of six main numbers has ever come up twice. Surprisingly, a repeat would not have been unusual. There are 13,983,816 combinations, but any pair of draws could match. We calculated the exact chance that 4,462 random draws contain at least one repeated combination as 1 minus the product of (1 − i/13,983,816) for i from 0 to 4,461. That comes to 50.9%, a coin flip. It hasn't happened yet, and even if it did, it would say nothing about the next draw.

Consecutive numbers are normal

Many players avoid picking two numbers in a row because it "doesn't look random." In fact, 49.0% of all 6/49 draws have included at least one consecutive pair, such as 22 and 23. The exact probability is about 49.5%, so roughly every second draw has one.

Numbers repeat from draw to draw

In 56.3% of draws, at least one number from the previous draw came up again. Chance predicts about 56%. A number appearing in back-to-back draws is ordinary, not a sign it's "running hot."

Birthday-only draws are rare

Players who pick only dates use numbers from 1 to 31. All six winning numbers fell in that range in just 4.9% of draws, close to the 5.3% expected. The other 95% of draws included at least one number from 32 to 49. That doesn't make a birthday ticket less likely to win. It does mean that when a birthday-range draw happens, the jackpot is more likely to be shared.

The longest drought

The longest any number has gone without being drawn in Lotto 6/49 is 79 consecutive draws. The number 7 was drawn on May 9, 1998 and didn't appear again until February 13, 1999, about nine months later. During a run like that, it's easy to believe a number is "due." But the draw has no memory. In the draw after its 79th straight miss, 7 had exactly the same chance as every other number, about 1 in 8 of being among the six. Long gaps happen somewhere in any long record. We measured, for each of the 49 numbers, the most consecutive draws it has ever missed. The shortest of those personal records is 35 draws, the median is 50, and 10 of the 49 numbers have at some point missed 60 or more draws in a row. Number 7's 79 is the longest, but it isn't on its own. (Method: for each number, we counted the draws between consecutive appearances across all 4,462 draws and took the maximum.)

Lotto Max: three eras, one story

Lotto Max is harder to summarise because its rules have changed. Our archive treats it as three separate eras:

Number rangeDatesDrawsChi-square (df)
1 to 49Sep 25, 2009 – May 10, 201950350.6 (48)
1 to 50May 14, 2019 – Apr 10, 202672233.4 (49)
1 to 52Apr 14, 2026 – Oct 23, 20265638.6 (51)

For its first decade, Lotto Max was drawn once a week, on Fridays. OLG's 2009 launch announcement says "Draws will be held on Friday nights." The Tuesday draw began on May 14, 2019, the same day the range grew to 1 to 50, according to BCLC's announcement of the change. In each era, the chi-square value is close to its degrees of freedom, which is what a fair draw produces. None of the three eras shows meaningful bias toward any number.

Mixing eras produces misleading statistics. Over all 1,281 draws, 51 has appeared only 8 times and 52 only 10 times. That isn't because they're "cold." Those numbers have only existed since April 2026. Any all-time Lotto Max frequency table that doesn't separate eras makes the highest numbers look unlucky. For the record, the all-time leaders are 28 (207 times), and 19 and 7 (205 each). In the current 1-to-52 era, with only 56 draws so far, 43 leads with 13 appearances and 20 trails with 2. In a sample that small, those figures will change quickly.

Other Lotto Max facts from the archive:

  • No set of seven main numbers has ever repeated.
  • 62% of draws have included a consecutive pair. With seven numbers drawn instead of six, pairs are more common than in 6/49.
  • 69.9% of draws repeated at least one number from the previous draw.
  • The lowest total was 76 (October 26, 2018), and the highest was 294 (January 26, 2021).
  • The longest drought was 62 consecutive draws: 12 was drawn on May 3, 2019 and not again until December 13, 2019.

What this record does not contain

Our archive holds the winning numbers. Our record of advertised jackpots only starts in October 2026, so this article doesn't rank the biggest jackpots or longest rollover runs in history. We would rather leave those out than repeat figures we can't check against our own data. For how a recent capped run played out, see what happens when the Lotto Max jackpot hits $90 million.

The takeaway

Forty-four years of draws make one point again and again: the results look exactly like chance. Some numbers lead and some lag, then they swap places. Strange-looking draws happen at the rate the arithmetic predicts. Long droughts end with no warning. That's reassuring, because it means the draws are fair, and freeing, because it means no chart, system or "overdue" list can improve your ticket. Pick numbers you enjoy, keep to a budget, and if you'd like to explore the data yourself, the Lotto 6/49 and Lotto Max results pages let you filter by period and number range.
