Hot numbers are a headline, not a model

Written by NextGen, British Columbia · Last updated October 2026

Open almost any lotto helper and you will see a red list of "hot" balls and a blue list of "overdue" balls. The marketing story is that the universe keeps a tab. The mathematical story is smaller: if you look at a short random sample, some values appear more often than others. That is required by chance, not forbidden by it.

Independence, in one paragraph

Canadian national draws are designed and audited so that each draw is independent of the previous one. The machine does not check last week's results. The probability that 17 is drawn tonight does not go up because 17 "is due", and it does not go up because 17 appeared twice last month. After the draw is certified, the next draw starts from exactly the same rules. WCLC puts it plainly on its own game pages: "The frequency that a number has been drawn in the past has no bearing on when it will be drawn in the future."

What a frequency table actually measures

Suppose you watch 40 draws of Lotto 6/49. Each draw shows six main numbers, so you have 240 number-appearances spread across 49 possible balls. If every ball were perfectly even you would see about 240 ÷ 49 ≈ 4.9 appearances each. You will not see that. Some balls will have 2 appearances, some will have 8. People screenshot the 8 and call it hot.

How much spread is normal? Each ball has a 6-in-49 chance of appearing in a draw, so over 40 draws the standard deviation of its count is √(40 × 6/49 × 43/49) ≈ 2.1. About 95% of balls should land within two standard deviations of the average — roughly 1 to 9 appearances. Our 6/49 archive and Lotto Max archive print this "normal band" next to the real counts, and the real counts sit inside it about as often as chance says they should.

Run the same experiment on the next 40 draws and many labels will flip. That flip is the point. A persistent edge would require faulty equipment, and lottery corporations test and audit their draw equipment specifically to rule that out. A hobby website counting last month's results is not that test.

The gambler's fallacy and the hot-hand story

Two opposite stories get sold to the same player:

  • Gambler's fallacy: "12 has not come up for a while, so it must come up soon."
  • Hot-hand story: "12 keeps coming up, so stick with 12."

They cannot both be general laws, and in an independent draw neither is. Both give a feeling of having a process. A process can be comforting, but the ticket still has the same 1 in 13,983,816 jackpot probability on 6/49. See odds explained.

Why "overdue" numbers are not owed anything

With seven numbers drawn from 52, any particular Lotto Max number misses a given draw with probability 45/52, about 87%. The chance it misses ten draws in a row is (45/52)10, about 24%. In other words, at any moment roughly a quarter of all Lotto Max numbers will look "ten draws overdue" for no reason at all. Long gaps are a normal feature of random sequences, not a debt waiting to be repaid.

Why we still publish counts

Hiding the table does not stop people wanting it. A labelled table, shown with the range chance produces and a reminder that it predicts nothing, is more honest than an unlabelled "prediction". When the archive is short, the hottest ball is almost meaningless; as the archive grows, counts crowd toward the same average and the apparent "strategy" fades away by itself.

How this site will not use the table

  • We will not say a number or line is "due".
  • We will not generate "suggested" number lines from any model or algorithm. No method can make a better line for a random draw.
  • We will not publish a near-identical page after every draw with new digits and the same commentary.
