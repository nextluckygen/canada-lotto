What happens when the Lotto Max jackpot hits $90 million

Lotto Max jackpots don't grow forever. Since April 2026 the main jackpot stops at $90 million, and the extra money goes into more prizes rather than one bigger one. This guide explains how the cap works, what MaxMillions and MaxPlus have to do with it, and what a real capped run looked like in October 2026.

The short version

  • The Lotto Max jackpot starts at $10 million and can grow to a maximum of $90 million, according to WCLC's game page.
  • Once the jackpot is over $50 million, MaxMillions prizes of $1 million each are added to the draw.
  • The jackpot and MaxMillions both keep growing until the jackpot reaches $90 million. After that only MaxMillions grow: each further rollover adds more $1 million prizes instead of a bigger jackpot.
  • MaxPlus is separate. Every draw has as many $100,000 MaxPlus prizes as there are millions in the jackpot, so a $25 million jackpot comes with 25 MaxPlus prizes.
  • Unwon MaxMillions prizes carry over to the next draw. Your odds of winning the main jackpot are the same 1 in 33,446,140 per $6 play whatever the jackpot size.

Why there is a cap at all

A lottery jackpot grows because nobody matched all the numbers and the money set aside for the top prize rolls over to the next draw. With no ceiling, a long run of rollovers could produce a single prize of several hundred million dollars. That is what happens in the US multi-state games. Canada's lottery corporations chose differently. When Lotto Max launched in September 2009, OLG's launch announcement said the jackpot would grow until won or until it reached $50 million. After that, top-prize money would go to a series of extra MaxMillions prizes of $1 million each, "each with its own individual draw and set of winning numbers."

There are practical reasons too. A capped jackpot keeps the top prize within a range that national sales can reliably support. The cap has been raised over time. It was $50 million at launch in 2009. An OLG release from May 2019 says it was raised to $60 million in 2015 and to $70 million in May 2019, when the Tuesday draw was added. The current rules come from the April 2026 redesign, which set the price at $6 for four selections from 1 to 52 and a jackpot range of $10 million to $90 million.

How MaxMillions work under the current rules

WCLC's Lotto Max page puts it in three sentences. MaxMillions prizes start when the jackpot is over $50 million. MaxMillions and the jackpot both keep growing until the jackpot hits $90 million. After that, only MaxMillions grow.

Each MaxMillions prize is a separate set of seven numbers drawn for that one prize. To win it, one of your lines must match all seven numbers of a MaxMillions selection exactly. There is no partial prize, and there is no bonus number. If more than one ticket matches the same MaxMillions selection, the $1 million is shared. If nobody matches it, that prize carries over to the next draw. So in a long capped run, each draw contains MaxMillions prizes carried over from the last draw plus any new ones added by that draw's sales.

Each MaxMillions selection has the same odds as the jackpot: WCLC lists 1 in 33,446,140 per play. With 20 MaxMillions selections in a draw, a single play has 20 separate one-in-33-million chances. That is still a very long shot, about 1 in 1.67 million, but it is a real improvement on a draw with no MaxMillions.

A note on the official wording. In October 2026, the footnote on WCLC's Lotto Max page still describes an older arrangement, where the jackpot "splits" into a $50 million main prize plus a variable number of $1 million prizes. That doesn't match the $90 million cap described in the main text of the same page, or the jackpots actually advertised. We follow the main game description and the observed draws, and we will update this guide if WCLC clarifies the footnote.

MaxPlus: the other set of extra prizes

MaxPlus arrived with the April 2026 redesign and is easy to confuse with MaxMillions. The difference:

Prize$1,000,000 each$100,000 each

To winMatch all 7 numbers of one selectionMatch all 7 numbers of one selection

Because MaxPlus is tied to the jackpot amount, MaxPlus stops growing when the jackpot reaches $90 million. Our records show 90 MaxPlus prizes in that case. From then on, extra rollovers show up only as more MaxMillions.

On October 20 it stayed at $90 million: the jackpot and MaxPlus were frozen, and the rollover went into MaxMillions instead, up to 20. The $90 million jackpot was then won in the October 20 draw by a ticket bought in Alberta, according to OLG as reported by Global News. It was the largest jackpot ever offered in Canada. The next advertised jackpot, for October 23, was back down to $20 million, with no MaxMillions because it was under $50 million again, and 20 MaxPlus prizes. Two cautions about reading this table. First, the step sizes ($5 million, two MaxMillions) are what was advertised in this run. They depend on sales, and the operators set each draw's figures, so other runs may move differently. Second, our jackpot records only start in October 2026, so this is one run, not a long-term pattern.

What the cap means for players

Your jackpot odds don't change

The odds of matching the seven jackpot numbers depend only on the number range and the number of lines. For Lotto Max that is 133,784,560 possible lines, or 1 in 33,446,140 per $6 play of four lines. A $90 million jackpot is no easier to win than a $10 million one. Our odds calculator shows the derivation step by step.

More tickets are sold, so sharing is more likely

Big jackpots sell more tickets. The chance that any one ticket matches is unchanged, but with more tickets in play it becomes more likely that two or more people match the same set and split the prize. That applies to the jackpot and to each $1 million MaxMillions prize.

The extra value goes into extra prizes

The value of a capped draw is spread out. Instead of one larger jackpot, a capped draw adds more MaxMillions selections, and each one is another separate chance at $1 million. Whether that makes a ticket "worth it" is a personal call. On average, every Lotto Max ticket returns less than its price: WCLC states that at least 48% of sales go to the prize fund, and the rest covers costs and provincial revenue. Treat any ticket as entertainment with a set budget. See responsible play.

Check every MaxMillions selection

In a draw with 20 MaxMillions prizes, there are 20 extra seven-number sets on top of the main draw and the MaxPlus sets. Checking those by eye is tiring and easy to get wrong. Scan the ticket with the official app or a retailer's ticket checker, which checks every prize category at once. Our claims guide explains how.

Common questions

Does the jackpot roll back to $10 million after a win?

The minimum jackpot is $10 million, but the jackpot advertised for the draw after a win depends on projected sales and any carried-over money. In October 2026 it restarted at $20 million. Check the official figure before each draw rather than assuming a starting amount.

What happens to MaxMillions nobody wins?

WCLC says unwon MaxMillions prizes carry over to the next draw. Once the jackpot is won and falls below $50 million, MaxMillions stop being offered until the next run passes $50 million again. The October 23 draw, after the October 20 jackpot was won, had none.

Is there a cap on Lotto 6/49 too?

Lotto 6/49 works differently. Its Classic Draw jackpot is a fixed $5 million, and the Gold Ball draw has its own jackpot that grows until the gold ball is drawn. See the Gold Ball guide.

If I win the jackpot and a MaxMillions prize, do I get both?

Each prize category is checked separately, so a line can in principle win in more than one category if it matches more than one selection. The game conditions for your region are the final authority on how prizes are combined.
