Lotto 6/49: six numbers from 1 to 49

The number of ways to choose 6 different numbers from 49, ignoring order, is the combination C(49, 6):

49 × 48 × 47 × 46 × 45 × 44 ÷ (6 × 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1) = 13,983,816.

So one 6/49 line has a 1 in 13,983,816 chance of matching all six Classic Draw numbers — exactly the figure on WCLC's official prize table. The bonus number is drawn from the remaining 43 balls and only matters for the 5-of-6-plus-bonus and 2-of-6-plus-bonus tiers. It is not a second chance at the jackpot.

That probability is in the same neighbourhood as flipping a fair coin and getting heads 24 times in a row (224 = 16,777,216). Jackpots feel common in the news because millions of tickets are sold across the country. Your ticket is still one combination.

Lotto 6/49 also includes the Gold Ball draw, which works completely differently: one ticket number is drawn from all Gold Ball selections sold, so your odds there are one in the number of selections issued for that draw. See how the Gold Ball works.

Lotto Max: seven numbers from 1 to 52 (since April 2026)

Lotto Max changed format in April 2026. Each $6 play now contains four selections of seven numbers from 1 to 52 (previously three selections from 1 to 50 for $5).

C(52, 7) = 52 × 51 × 50 × 49 × 48 × 47 × 46 ÷ 7! = 133,784,560 possible lines.

One selection therefore has a 1 in 133,784,560 chance of matching all seven main numbers. Because a $6 play contains four different selections, the chance per play is four times better: 133,784,560 ÷ 4 = 1 in 33,446,140, the figure published by WCLC and OLG. That is roughly the chance of 25 heads in a row. The odds of winning any Lotto Max prize are about 1 in 5.8 per play, mostly from the lower tiers (three numbers wins a Free Play).

The $100,000 MaxPlus draws and $1 million MaxMillions draws use the same 7-of-52 format, so each one has the same 1 in 33,446,140 odds per play. More on that in our Lotto Max and MaxMillions guide.

What a "system" can and cannot do

  • Cannot: raise the probability of a specific combination. Every combination is equally likely in every independent draw — including 1-2-3-4-5-6.
  • Can: change how a prize would be shared. Popular patterns — birthdays (1–31), straight lines on the slip, 7-14-21-28 — are chosen by more players. If such a pattern is drawn, more winners split the pool. Unpopular combinations are not drawn more often, but you would share less if they were.
  • Can: tell you the price of more combinations. Two plays give twice the chance at twice the cost. That is arithmetic, not a loophole.

Expected value, briefly

A ticket's expected value is the sum of each prize multiplied by its probability, minus the price. Lottery prize funds return well under the ticket price on average — WCLC's Lotto Max conditions, for example, set the prize fund at a minimum of 48% of sales. That is by design: a lotto ticket is priced as entertainment, not as an investment. Rollover jackpots raise the top prize and nudge the expected value up, but they do not turn a typical ticket into a good financial bet.

Why this site still shows frequencies

People like looking at history, and that is a reasonable hobby. It becomes a problem when a chart is sold as a forecast. The frequency tables in our Lotto Max and Lotto 6/49 archives describe a short sample and are shown next to the range you would expect from pure chance. Over a long series each number appears about equally often; in a few dozen draws, some numbers look "hot" and some look "cold". That is what random data does — see hot and cold numbers.
