Editorial policy

lottohelper.ca is written and maintained by NextGen, an independent publisher in British Columbia. This page explains where our information comes from, how it is checked, and what happens when we get something wrong.

Our sources

Every factual claim about the games, prizes, claims or taxes is checked against a primary source. That means the organizations that run the games or set the rules:

  • Western Canada Lottery Corporation (WCLC): game pages, game conditions, prize structures, claim procedures and official winning numbers. Our results archive reads WCLC's public winning-number pages.
  • BCLC: BC claim limits, prize payout locations, ID requirements and product announcements.
  • OLG and Loto-Québec: Ontario and Quebec rules, Encore, and the history of the national games.
  • Canada Revenue Agency (CRA): how lottery prizes and the income earned on them are treated for tax.
  • Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre: current lottery and prize scams.

News reports are used only for background, such as the date of an old rule change. When they disagree with an operator, the operator wins. We do not use other lotto-tips websites as sources. Each guide ends with a Sources list linking the pages it relies on, and the month we last checked them.

How we check a guide

  1. Research. Read the current official page and, where it exists, the formal game conditions document, which is more precise than marketing pages.
  2. Calculate. Odds and expected values are worked out from the published rules and checked against the operator's odds tables. If our figure differs from theirs, we find out why before publishing.
  3. Check against the data. Statistics on our results pages and in data articles are computed from the full draw archive by the same code that builds the site, so the numbers update when new draws are added.
  4. Review. Before a guide is published or substantially changed, every number, limit, date and phone number is re-checked against its source link.
  5. Date it. Each guide shows when it was last updated. Rules change. Lotto Max changed price, number range and prizes in April 2026, for example. Guides are reviewed again when an operator announces a change.

Where official sources contradict each other, we say so in the article instead of picking the convenient version. Where a question needs professional advice, such as a large prize, estate planning or investment tax, the guide says that too.

What we will not publish

  • Predictions. No "numbers to play", hot-number tips, lucky-number systems or claims that a pattern makes a win more likely. Lotto draws are independent. Our statistics describe the past and are labelled that way.
  • Paid placement. No sponsored articles, ticket-reseller links or affiliate deals with lottery sellers. Advertising is served by Google AdSense, and advertisers have no say over content.
  • Invented figures. Jackpots are shown as advertised by the operator. If a figure is not available, we leave it out instead of estimating it.

Corrections

If something here disagrees with an official source, the official source is correct. When we find or are told about an error:

  • factual errors in a guide (a wrong limit, date, odds figure or phone number) are fixed as soon as they are confirmed, and the "Last updated" date changes;
  • if the error could have affected a reader's decision, such as a claim deadline, we add a short correction note to the article explaining what changed;
  • results errors are corrected from the official winning-number page, and the archive is rebuilt.

To report an error, use the details on our contact page. Please include the page address and the official source you are comparing it with.
