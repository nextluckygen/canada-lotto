The odds do not care which numbers you like

This page exists because a lot of lotto sites imply that a strategy exists. There is a strategy for not sharing a jackpot. There is no strategy that makes a jackpot more likely.

6/49: six numbers from 1–49

The number of ways to choose 6 different numbers from 49 is the combination C(49, 6):

49 × 48 × 47 × 46 × 45 × 44 ÷ (6 × 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1) = 13,983,816.

One standard 6/49 combination has a 1 in 13,983,816 chance of matching all six main numbers. The bonus ball is drawn from the leftover 43 numbers and only matters for some lower prize tiers. It does not give you a second jackpot.

That figure is in the same neighbourhood as flipping a fair coin and getting heads about 24 times in a row (224 = 16,777,216). Jackpots feel common in the news because a whole country is buying tickets. Your individual ticket is still that one combination.

Lotto Max: seven numbers from 1–50

C(50, 7) = 50 × 49 × 48 × 47 × 46 × 45 × 44 ÷ 7! = 33,294,800.

One Max combination is about 1 in 33.3 million for the main jackpot line. Extra lines on the same ticket are separate combinations. They improve your chance only because you bought more combinations, not because the machine became friendlier.

What a “system” can and cannot do

  • Cannot do: raise the probability of a specific 6-number or 7-number set. Every unused combination is equally likely on the next independent draw.
  • Can do: change how a prize would be shared. Popular patterns — birthdays (1–31), 7-14-21-28 — are chosen by more players. If that pattern hits, more people split the pot. Unpopular combinations do not hit more often.
  • Can do: tell you the price of more combinations. Two tickets are twice the chance and twice the cost. That is arithmetic, not a loophole.

Expected value, quickly

A ticket’s expected value is the sum of (prize × probability) minus the price. For national jackpot games that number is almost always below the ticket price. That is by design. A lotto ticket is priced as entertainment, not as an investment.

Rollover jackpots raise the top prize and therefore raise expected value a little. They do not turn a typical ticket into a good financial bet.

Why this site still shows frequencies

People like looking at history. That is a reasonable hobby. It becomes a problem when a chart is sold as a forecast. The frequency table is a description of a short sample. Over a long series each number should appear about equally often. In a window of a few dozen draws, some numbers look “hot” and some look “cold.” That is what random data does. See Hot and cold.
