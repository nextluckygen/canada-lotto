Biggest Canadian lottery wins: Lotto Max and Lotto 6/49 record jackpots

Written by NextGen, British Columbia · Last updated October 2026

This page lists some of the largest jackpot wins in Canadian lottery history. Every row links to the lottery corporation's own announcement or to a news report of it. If an official source hasn't published a detail, such as the store or whether the numbers were a Quick Pick, the cell says not announced rather than guessing.

How this table is built

  • Only sourced facts. Each row cites a release from OLG, BCLC or Loto-Québec, or a national news report quoting one of them. If we couldn't confirm a detail, we left it out.
  • "Not announced" means the source we found doesn't say. It doesn't mean the information is secret. Lottery corporations often release the store and the winner's story only after the prize is claimed.
  • Amounts are the jackpot, not including other prizes on the same ticket unless the row says so. Where a jackpot was shared, the row says how many tickets shared it.
  • Winners are identified by community only. The original releases name the winners. We leave names out because they add nothing to the record.
  • This isn't a complete list of every jackpot of this size. It's the set of large wins we could document to the standard above, and we add rows as sources are confirmed.

What the records show

The record has moved up with the cap. Lotto Max's biggest wins track the jackpot cap almost exactly. The first $70 million jackpot was won in January 2020, after the May 2019 rule change. The first $80 million jackpot was won in September 2024, and the October 20, 2026 draw produced a winning ticket for a $90 million jackpot under the cap introduced in April 2026. A Lotto Max jackpot can't grow past its cap, so each new record needs a new cap first. For how the cap works, see what happens when the Lotto Max jackpot hits $90 million.

Lotto 6/49's record has stood since 2015. The $64 million jackpot from October 17, 2015 is still the largest in 6/49's history. The game was later redesigned around a fixed $5 million Classic jackpot and the Gold Ball draw, so a rollover of that size can't happen under the current rules. See how the Gold Ball draw works.

"Largest" depends on what you count. BCLC called the May 2025 Surrey win the largest jackpot ever won by a single individual in Canada. OLG called the December 2025 London win Canada's largest lottery payout, because the ticket also won the second prize through Combination Play. Then the October 2026 draw offered a $90 million jackpot, the largest ever. Each claim is accurate on its own terms. When you read "biggest ever", check whether it means the largest jackpot, the largest payout, or the largest amount won by one person.

Big prizes can go unclaimed. The $70 million ticket from June 28, 2022, sold in Scarborough, was never presented before the one-year deadline. OLG said almost 2,700 people contacted it claiming to have lost the ticket, and none of those claims succeeded. The money went back into the prize pool for national games.

Quick Pick and chosen numbers win at the same rate. Several winners in the table used Quick Pick, but that reflects how popular Quick Pick is, not any advantage. Every combination has the same chance in every draw. The odds are worked out on our odds calculator.

Retailer commissions

Stores that sell big winning tickets receive a commission. Loto-Québec's releases for both $70 million wins in 2020 say the store received 1% of the prize, or $700,000. Other provinces' releases cited here don't give a figure, so we don't state one for them.
