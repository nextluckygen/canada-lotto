Lotto Max vs Lotto 6/49 vs Daily Grand: what your money actually buys

Written by NextGen, British Columbia · Last updated November 2026

Canada has three national lotto games, and they're built very differently. Lotto Max sells the biggest jackpots. Lotto 6/49 pairs a fixed $5 million jackpot with a Gold Ball draw that has a winner every time. Daily Grand pays its top prize for life. This guide puts the official numbers side by side, works out what a dollar buys in each game, and explains what the headline figures leave out.

Facts, not tips. Every draw is random and no game or number choice changes that. The comparisons below use the official prize tables and odds published by WCLC, plus our own arithmetic, which we show. They describe the games; they aren't a reason to spend more.

How each game works

Lotto Max: big jackpot, many extra prize draws

Since April 2026, a $6 Lotto Max play gives you four lines of seven numbers from 1 to 52. Seven numbers and a bonus are drawn. The jackpot starts at $10 million and grows when no one wins it, up to a $90 million cap. Every draw also has MaxPlus prizes of $100,000, one for each million in the jackpot. Once the jackpot reaches $50 million, extra MaxMillions draws worth $1 million each are added. MaxPlus and MaxMillions prizes need an exact seven-number match on one line. Our guide to Lotto Max, MaxPlus and MaxMillions covers the details.

Lotto 6/49: fixed jackpot plus a guaranteed winner

A $3 Lotto 6/49 play is one line of six numbers from 1 to 49 for the Classic Draw, plus a 10-digit Gold Ball number printed by the terminal. The Classic jackpot is always $5 million and doesn't roll over. In the Gold Ball draw, one Gold Ball number is picked from all those sold for the draw, so someone always wins. That winner gets $1 million, or the Gold Ball jackpot if the single gold ball comes out of a drum of white balls. The Gold Ball jackpot starts at $10 million and rises by $2 million each draw it isn't won. For Wednesday, November 4, 2026, WCLC advertised a $34 million Gold Ball jackpot with 18 balls left in the drum. See how the Gold Ball draw works.

Daily Grand: smaller odds, paid for life

A $3 Daily Grand play is five numbers from 1 to 49 plus one Grand Number from 1 to 7. Matching all five and the Grand Number wins $1,000 a day for life, or a $7,000,000 lump sum. Matching all five without the Grand Number wins $25,000 a year for life, or $500,000. All the lower prizes are fixed amounts, and matching only the Grand Number wins a Free Play. If more than one ticket wins the top or second prize, WCLC says the winners split the lump sum for that prize. Our guide to lump sum vs annuity explains the for-life option and its tax detail.

Comparing the same $6 in each game

Lotto Max costs twice as much per play as the other two, so a fair comparison uses the same amount of money. Here is what $6 buys, using the official odds:

The pattern is clear. Per dollar, Lotto 6/49 and Daily Grand give you about five times the chance of winning their top prize that Lotto Max does. Lotto Max's top prize is bigger, starting at twice the 6/49 Classic jackpot and reaching up to 18 times it.

Counting Lotto Max's extra $1 million draws

Each MaxMillions draw has the same 1 in 33,446,140 odds per play as the jackpot, so a night with many of them gives you more chances at a $1 million-plus prize. Two real examples from our Lotto Max results archive, counting the jackpot plus each MaxMillions draw:

  • Friday, November 6, 2026: WCLC advertised a $50 million jackpot with 2 MaxMillions prizes. That's 3 prizes of $1 million or more, so the chance that one $6 play wins at least one is about 3 in 33,446,140, or 1 in 11.1 million.
  • Tuesday, October 20, 2026: the jackpot was at its $90 million cap with 20 MaxMillions prizes. That's 21 prizes of $1 million or more: about 1 in 1.6 million per $6 play.

On a capped night, Lotto Max gives the best odds of the three at winning at least $1 million per dollar spent, even though its jackpot odds are the longest. On an ordinary night with no MaxMillions, it gives the worst. Lotto Max also has MaxPlus: 50 prizes of $100,000 on November 6, and 90 on October 20.

Fixed prizes and shared prizes

The games also differ in how much of the prize table is guaranteed.

  • Daily Grand pays every prize as a fixed amount, from the Free Play up to $1,000 a day. Only the top two prizes can be split, and only if more than one ticket wins them in the same draw.
  • Lotto 6/49 has a fixed $5 million jackpot and fixed small prizes ($10 for three numbers, $5 for two plus the bonus, and a Free Play for two). The middle tiers, from four numbers to five plus the bonus, are shares of a Pools Fund. The money set aside for each tier is divided among everyone who hits it.
  • Lotto Max has the fewest fixed prizes: $20 for four numbers or three plus the bonus, and a Free Play for three. The jackpot and every tier from four plus the bonus up are shared. A big jackpot that several tickets match is split.

Fixed prizes make a game easier to understand: you know what a given match pays before the draw. Shared prizes vary. When many people play the same popular numbers, such as dates or patterns on the slip, a shared prize can be split many ways. Choosing less popular numbers doesn't change your chance of winning, but it can affect how many people you'd share with. Our guide to hot and cold numbers explains why past draws don't affect future ones.

What the prize tables show about value

Daily Grand is the one game where every prize is a known amount, so you can work out its average return from WCLC's table. Using the lump-sum values ($7 million and $500,000), assuming no shared prizes, and counting a Free Play as worth its $3 price:

So, on average, a $3 Daily Grand play returns about $1.52 in prizes, or roughly 51 cents per dollar. Almost half of that comes from the two top prizes, which very few players will ever see. The prizes most players actually win (the $4, the Free Play and the $10) add up to about 55 cents per play. WCLC describes its odds as approximate, so treat these as rounded figures.

The same exercise can't be finished for Lotto Max or Lotto 6/49, because their shared tiers depend on sales and the number of winners in each draw. What we can say from the fixed parts of the tables:

  • For Lotto 6/49, the $5 million Classic jackpot is worth about 36 cents per $3 play, assuming one winner, and the fixed small prizes and Free Play about 60 cents more. The Pools Fund tiers and the Gold Ball draw are on top of that.
  • For Lotto Max, the jackpot's average value per $6 play ranges from about 30 cents at $10 million to about $2.69 at $90 million, if one ticket wins it alone. The fixed $20 prizes and Free Play add about $1.41. Shared tiers, MaxPlus and MaxMillions come on top.

Two things follow. A Lotto Max play is worth more on average when the jackpot is high, but big jackpots also bring more sales, so the chance of sharing goes up. And for all three games, the average return is well under the ticket price, as with any lottery. Proceeds fund the programs and services each province supports.

Other differences that matter

How prizes are paid

Lotto Max and Lotto 6/49 prizes are paid in one lump sum. Daily Grand's top two prizes give the winner a choice between payments for life and a lump sum. Lottery prizes aren't taxed as income in Canada, but WCLC notes there are tax implications if a Daily Grand winner chooses the annuity. See are lottery winnings taxed in Canada?

When you can play

With draws on six days of the week between them (Monday and Thursday, Tuesday and Friday, Wednesday and Saturday), the three games never share a draw night. Sales cut-off times differ by region; our guide to buying tickets online lists them.

Quick summary

  • Biggest possible prize: Lotto Max, up to $90 million plus MaxMillions.
  • Best top-prize odds per dollar: Daily Grand (1 in 6.67 million per $6), just ahead of Lotto 6/49's Classic jackpot (1 in 6.99 million per $6).
  • Most certain prize amounts: Daily Grand, where every prize is a fixed amount.
  • A winner every draw: Lotto 6/49's Gold Ball draw.
  • Best odds at $1 million or more on a big night: Lotto Max when the jackpot is capped and many MaxMillions are added.
  • For all three: the average return is less than the ticket price. Set a budget and treat tickets as entertainment. Our odds calculator shows what a year of play looks like.
