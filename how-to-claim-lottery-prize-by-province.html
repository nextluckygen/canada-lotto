How to claim a lottery prize in Ontario, Quebec, Alberta and Atlantic Canada

Written by NextGen, British Columbia · Last updated October 2026

Lotto Max and Lotto 6/49 are national games, but there's no national prize office. You claim from the lottery corporation in the region where the ticket was sold, and each one has its own limits, offices and paperwork. This guide covers Ontario, Quebec, Alberta and the four Atlantic provinces one at a time, using each corporation's own published rules. For British Columbia and the rest of Western Canada, see our BC and Western Canada claim guide.

Before you claim, wherever you are

Some advice comes from every corporation in this guide, so it's worth doing before you decide where to go:

  • Sign the ticket. OLG, Atlantic Lottery and BCLC tell players to sign their paper tickets, and WCLC asks for your name on the front and in the contact section on the back. Atlantic Lottery says filling in the back of the ticket "helps to protect you if your ticket is lost or stolen."
  • Check it through an official channel. Use the corporation's app, a self-serve ticket checker or the retailer's terminal. OLG's statement on the unclaimed $70 million prize noted that the ticket was never checked through any of these.
  • Note the deadline. For Lotto Max and Lotto 6/49, each corporation in this guide gives one year from the draw date. Loto-Québec puts it as 365 days.
  • Have government photo ID ready. Every corporation in this guide requires ID that matches the name on the ticket.

Lottery prizes themselves aren't taxed in Canada, though income you later earn on the money is. Atlantic Lottery's claim page says this directly. For more detail, see are lottery winnings taxed in Canada?

Ontario (OLG)

The Ontario Lottery and Gaming Corporation runs lottery sales in Ontario. Its process centres on an online claim form, with in-person claims for the largest prizes.

Small prizes: under $1,000

OLG's online claim form says prizes under $1,000 can be redeemed at retailers. The form also accepts smaller prizes if you have a reason not to use a store, and lists examples: you don't live in Ontario, you couldn't find a retailer to pay, or the ticket is damaged.

Larger prizes: the online claim form

For larger prizes you start with OLG's online claim form, which OLG says takes about 10 minutes. You upload images of the front and back of the ticket and the front and back of your government-issued photo ID. What happens next depends on the amount. OLG says it will either mail a cheque, usually in about a week, ask you for more information, or help you book an in-person appointment at the OLG Prize Centre.

$50,000 and up: an interview

OLG's form says that "claims of $50,000 or more require an interview with our team." The big winners in our table of record wins were presented with their prizes at the OLG Prize Centre in Toronto or at winner celebrations.

Groups

The form asks whether the claim is for a group. If it is, OLG says group claims must be submitted by the group leader. By going ahead, you certify that you are that leader. Agree within the group who the leader is before anyone fills in the form.

Publicity and personal information

OLG's notice of collection on the claim form lists "publication of personal information as further described on this form" among the purposes for which it collects claimant details. Expect a large win to be announced, and read the form's publicity terms before you submit it.

Help

OLG's general and prize-claim line is 1-800-387-0098, open 8 a.m. to midnight Eastern time, seven days a week, according to OLG's contact page. Chat is also available around the clock.

Quebec (Loto-Québec)

Loto-Québec's public material on claims is mostly in French and spread across several pages. Some of them blocked our checks, so this section sticks to what Loto-Québec states in its English press releases.

Where big prizes are claimed

Loto-Québec's winner releases name the claim centre for each large prize. The February 2020 $70 million Lotto Max win was claimed at "Loto-Québec's offices in Québec City." The October 2020 $70 million win was claimed at "Loto-Québec head office in Montréal." So large prizes are handled at Loto-Québec's own offices in both cities. Which office you use depends on where you are and on Loto-Québec's instructions.

First call: customer service

In its notices about unclaimed prizes, Loto-Québec asks ticket holders to call customer service at 1-866-611-5686. That's the safest first step for any prize you can't redeem at a store, because they will tell you the current thresholds and what to bring.

Checking tickets

Loto-Québec says you can check tickets "via lotoquebec.com and the Loto-Québec Lotteries app, or at retailers."

Deadline

Loto-Québec states that "winners have 365 days following the draw to claim their prize". Its own example shows how the day is counted: a $1,000,000 Extra prize from the January 6, 2024 draw had a claim deadline of January 5, 2025. Loto-Québec also publicises prizes over $100,000 that haven't been claimed after several weeks, and lists all unclaimed prizes of $100,000 or more on lotoquebec.com.

What retailers receive

Loto-Québec's 2020 releases both say the store that sold the $70 million ticket received a 1% commission, $700,000.

Not covered here: Loto-Québec's exact cash limits for stores and its claim-form thresholds. We couldn't confirm them on a Loto-Québec page we could access, so we've left them out rather than repeat figures from unofficial sites.

Alberta (WCLC)

In Alberta, lottery tickets are sold under the Western Canada Lottery Corporation, which also serves Saskatchewan, Manitoba and the territories. Its claim rules are set out by prize size.

Up to $1,000

WCLC says prizes up to $100 are validated automatically when scanned, and the retailer must pay or arrange payment. For prizes from $100.01 to $1,000, "retailers may choose not to pay prizes over $100" and may send you to another location. Players who bought through the Lotto Spot app show the barcode from the app's "My Tickets" section. WCLC says printed copies of app tickets "will NOT be accepted."

$1,001 and up: prize office or mail

For $1,001 and above, WCLC says to make an appointment with a prize office or claim by mail. It adds a warning: "Prizes may not be paid during your appointment." Offices work "by appointment only." Alberta has two:

  • St. Albert: 780-447-8616, 8:30 a.m. to 3:30 p.m.
  • Calgary: 403-663-8301, 8:30 a.m. to 3:30 p.m.

To claim by mail, contact Player Care first for mailing instructions. Print your name on the front of the ticket and in the contact section on the back, and keep a copy of both sides. WCLC suggests a tracked mailing option and states that it "IS NOT responsible for lost mail." WCLC publishes a separate Alberta claimant information form to send with mailed claims. You can also fill it in ahead of an appointment to speed things up.

Groups

WCLC says group claims need a completed Group Buying Agreement, and asks groups to contact Player Care for details.

Help

WCLC's Player Care line, from its unclaimed-prize page, is 1-800-665-3313 during regular business hours.

Atlantic Canada (Atlantic Lottery)

Atlantic Lottery serves New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, Prince Edward Island, and Newfoundland and Labrador. Its process gives the most options for mid-sized prizes, because banks take part.

Up to $250, or a bit more

Atlantic Lottery says prizes of $250 and under can be redeemed at lottery retailers, and that "some retail locations can redeem prizes up to and including $500 and some up to $999."

$250 to $24,999: Scotiabank, Moncton or online

Prizes in this range "can be redeemed at any Bank of Nova Scotia (Scotiabank) in Atlantic Canada or in person at Atlantic Lottery's Head Office in Moncton, NB." There's also an online Prize Claim Form for prizes up to $24,999. Atlantic Lottery says it can only be used if you checked the ticket on a ticket checker, a mobile device or alc.ca. A ticket already validated on a lottery terminal can't go through the online process, and you'll need to call instead. Online claims take three to five business days, and you must keep the original ticket until you're paid.

By mail

For prizes up to $1,000, Atlantic Lottery accepts mail claims at PO Box 5500, 922 Main St., Moncton, N.B., E1C 8W6. Send the original ticket, a copy of your ID, your address, and a phone number or email. For prizes over $1,000, contact Atlantic Lottery's prize office (details on its claim page) for instructions before mailing anything. Atlantic Lottery estimates six to eight weeks for mail claims.

$25,000 and up

Call Atlantic Lottery at 1-800-561-3942. Atlantic Lottery says it will put you in touch with its Winners Team.

What to bring

Atlantic Lottery's requirements are the most detailed of the corporations here:

  • One valid primary government ID, such as a driver's licence, passport, provincial or federal ID card, or permanent resident card. If you don't have one, two pieces of secondary ID, such as a birth certificate or an expired licence or passport.
  • Banking information, such as a void cheque.
  • The signed ticket and a completed Prize Claim Form.
  • Time. "Winner(s) must be present during the entire processing time," and a claim that arrives late in the day may not be paid the same day.

Atlantic Lottery says it won't keep copies of your ID after the prize is processed and paid. It also notes that claim volumes are highest on Mondays and on Tuesdays after a statutory holiday.

Groups

For groups of two or more, Atlantic Lottery says a Group Win Agreement form "must be completed by all members of the group." The group trustee signs the ticket and "should indicate 'In Trust' on the ticket." Prizes are paid only to the ticket holder or the group trustee.

Publicity

Atlantic Lottery's rule is explicit: "When claiming a prize of $25,000 and above, you are required to let us use your full name, city, province, prize amount, game won on, and a current photograph to publicize your winnings."

Tickets bought elsewhere

Atlantic Lottery's restrictions say tickets bought in other lottery jurisdictions "must be claimed from the address located on the reverse of the ticket." That matches the general rule: claim from the corporation that sold the ticket, not the one where you live.

Common mistakes

  • Waiting too long. A year passes quickly, and the deadline is strict. See the unclaimed-prizes guide for real examples.
  • Mailing a ticket with no copy. WCLC and Atlantic Lottery both tell you to keep a copy of your ticket, and WCLC accepts no responsibility for lost mail.
  • Claiming a group win as an individual. OLG, WCLC and Atlantic Lottery all publish a group process. Using it from the start avoids disputes later.
  • Trusting an unexpected "you've won" message. Check any such message against the lottery corporation's official site or phone line. Read our guide to lottery scams.
