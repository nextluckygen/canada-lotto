Unclaimed lottery prizes and ticket expiry in Canada, by region

Written by NextGen, British Columbia · Last updated October 2026

Every year, some winning tickets are never cashed. Some are lost, some are forgotten in a drawer, and some are never checked. In Canada the deadline for Lotto Max and Lotto 6/49 is about a year from the draw, and once it passes the prize is gone. This guide sets out the rules for each lottery corporation in their own words, explains where the money goes, and shows how to check whether a prize from your area is still waiting.

The deadline in each region

Lotto Max and Lotto 6/49 are national games, but each regional corporation publishes its own expiry rule. They all come to roughly one year. The exact wording matters if you're close to the end.

RegionCorporationDeadline for Lotto Max and 6/49, in the corporation's words
British ColumbiaBCLC"52 weeks from the draw date printed on their ticket"
Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, territoriesWCLCTickets "expire one year from the draw date"
OntarioOLGTerminal tickets such as Lotto Max and 6/49 "expire one year following the draw date on the ticket"
QuebecLoto-Québec"365 days following the draw"
New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, PEI, Newfoundland and LabradorAtlantic Lottery"One year from the draw date as indicated on the ticket"

How the last day is counted

Two official examples show the deadline is strict to the day, and even to the minute:

  • Ontario: the $70 million Lotto Max ticket from Tuesday, June 28, 2022 had to be validated or presented "on or before the deadline of 10:30 pm June 28, 2023", according to OLG.
  • Quebec: Loto-Québec gave a deadline of January 5, 2025 for a prize from the January 6, 2024 draw, and September 15, 2024 for a prize from the September 16, 2023 draw. In Loto-Québec's counting, the last day falls the day before the anniversary.

Don't plan to claim on the last day. Prize offices in several regions are appointment-only, and mail claims take weeks. Atlantic Lottery warns that "your prize may not be paid out the same day" and estimates six to eight weeks for mail claims. What matters for the deadline is when the corporation validates or receives the ticket, so leave plenty of margin.

Other games have other rules

The one-year rule is for draw games. Other products differ:

  • Scratch tickets. WCLC says Scratch & Win tickets "have an expiry date printed on the back of the ticket." Atlantic Lottery says Scratch'N Win expiry dates "are printed on the reverse of the ticket" and asks players to check them at purchase, adding that it "will not honour prizes for expired tickets."
  • Sports betting. WCLC gives Sport Select holders one year from the date the last selected game was played. Atlantic Lottery gives sports wagers 744 days from the purchase date.
  • Add-ons. WCLC lists Extra with the games that expire one year from the draw date.

The $70 million ticket nobody claimed

The best-known Canadian example is the Lotto Max jackpot from June 28, 2022. OLG's statement of August 10, 2023 sets out what happened:

  • The single winning ticket was sold at a retailer in Scarborough.
  • It was never validated at an OLG terminal or presented to OLG before the deadline.
  • OLG confirmed it was never checked at a self-serve ticket checker or through the OLG app.
  • Before the deadline, OLG's Customer Care Centre logged "almost 2,700 calls and emails from people claiming to have lost the ticket." OLG investigators reviewed each one, and none succeeded.
  • The $70 million "has been returned to the prize pool for future bonus games or promotions of national lottery games, namely, LOTTO MAX, LOTTO 6/49 and DAILY GRAND."

The 2,700 lost-ticket claims show the real problem. Without the physical ticket, or an online purchase record, a claim can't be proven. OLG's statement says it is "committed to paying the right prize to the rightful owner of a winning ticket—every time," and that standard has to be met with evidence.

Where unclaimed money goes

The corporations describe this in slightly different terms, but the pattern is consistent. Money from the national games goes back to national-game players, and money from regional games stays in the region.

  • OLG: the unclaimed $70 million was returned to the prize pool for bonus games or promotions of Lotto Max, Lotto 6/49 and Daily Grand.
  • WCLC: "Unclaimed prizes on national games are returned to players through guaranteed Jackpots and Bonus draws. Unclaimed prizes on regional games are returned to the member provinces and territories as revenue."
  • Loto-Québec: "Unclaimed prizes are redistributed to players as bonus prizes and special draws."
  • Atlantic Lottery: for games offered only by Atlantic Lottery, expired prizes go to "a special prize fund" and are "used for prizes in subsequent draws." Unclaimed prizes from the national games, which Atlantic Lottery lists as Lotto 6/49, Daily Grand, Lotto Max and certain scratch tickets, "are administered by the Interprovincial Lottery Corporation."
  • BCLC: we didn't find a BCLC page describing how its unclaimed prizes are used, so we don't describe it here.

None of these corporations say that unclaimed prizes are simply kept as profit from the national games. The extra Guaranteed $1 Million prizes and bonus draws you see advertised are among the ways that money comes back to players.

How to check for an unclaimed prize near you

Corporations publish lists of large unclaimed prizes. Each list works differently.

WCLC (Prairies and the North)

WCLC's "Unclaimed Prizes" page lists draw-game prizes over $10,000 that have gone unclaimed for three months or more, updated monthly. Wins are listed by city when the ticket was sold in a major city (Winnipeg, Regina, Saskatoon, Edmonton, Calgary, Yellowknife or Whitehorse), and otherwise by province or territory. When we checked the version last updated October 7, 2026, it listed several Alberta prizes. They included Lotto 6/49 Guaranteed $1 Million prizes sold in Calgary (December 6, 2025 draw) and Edmonton (December 27, 2025 and January 10, 2026 draws), and a $1,000,000 Lotto Max prize sold elsewhere in Alberta (December 9, 2025 draw). Under the one-year rule, the earliest of these expire in December 2026.

Loto-Québec

Loto-Québec says it tells ticket buyers when a prize over $100,000 still hasn't been claimed after several weeks. It also lists all unclaimed prizes of $100,000 or more in the Useful Information tab of the Lotteries section of lotoquebec.com. Its notices give the regional county municipality where the ticket was sold, the draw date, the winning selection and the exact claim deadline.

OLG

OLG's statement points players to the "Unclaimed Tickets" page on OLG.ca for information about other unclaimed tickets.

Atlantic Lottery

Atlantic Lottery says that when a ticket is nearing its expiry date, it uses social media, reminders at retail and news releases to get the word out, and asks people to check glove compartments, old jackets and hiding places.

Habits that stop it happening to you

OLG's unclaimed-prize statement lists four habits. They're simple and they work anywhere in Canada:

  1. Sign the ticket as soon as you buy it at a store.
  2. Keep it somewhere safe and memorable until the draw.
  3. Check it as soon as the draw is complete.
  4. Or buy online, where OLG says you'll be notified by email if the ticket wins.

We'd add two more that follow from the rules above:

  • Photograph both sides of the ticket. A photo isn't a valid claim. Corporations pay on the original ticket, and BCLC says only the original can be used for a group claim. But a photo records the draw date and serial number if you need to describe the ticket later.
  • Check old tickets against the archive. If you find a ticket and aren't sure of the draw, our number checker compares a line against every past draw. Then confirm through the official app or a retailer, and check the expiry date before you travel to a prize office.

Questions

Can a lottery corporation extend the deadline?

The published rules quoted in this guide set the deadline with no extension. WCLC's FAQ answers the "ticket in my junk drawer" case directly: "No. All lottery tickets have specific expiry dates and no prizes can be paid after that time."

I lost my ticket. Can I still claim?

The Scarborough case shows how hard this is. None of the almost 2,700 lost-ticket claims succeeded. If you bought online through a corporation's own site or app, the purchase is tied to your account, which is one practical advantage of buying that way.

I bought my ticket in another province. Whose deadline applies?

Claim from the corporation that sold the ticket. Its rules and deadline apply. Atlantic Lottery, for example, says tickets bought in other jurisdictions "must be claimed from the address located on the reverse of the ticket." See claiming a prize, province by province.
