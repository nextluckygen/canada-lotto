Buying lottery tickets online in Canada: the official site for each province

Written by NextGen, British Columbia · Last updated November 2026

You can buy Lotto Max, Lotto 6/49 and Daily Grand tickets online in Canada, but only from the lottery corporation that serves the province you are standing in. Each region has its own website or app, its own minimum age and its own rules for subscriptions and advance play. This guide lists the official option for every province and territory, explains how the location and age checks work, and covers the unofficial "ticket services" that the lottery corporations warn about.

Checked November 2026. Lottery corporations change their apps, payment methods and game line-ups from time to time. Every detail below is taken from the corporation's own website or rules, linked in the sources at the end. If something looks different when you visit, the corporation's site is the one to trust.

The short version

  • Canada has five regional lottery corporations: BCLC (British Columbia), WCLC (Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba and the three territories), OLG (Ontario), Loto-Québec and Atlantic Lottery (New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, Prince Edward Island and Newfoundland and Labrador).
  • Each one sells online only to people physically located in its region. A Toronto resident on holiday in Vancouver can't buy from OLG.ca, and can't buy from PlayNow either unless they are a B.C. resident with a PlayNow account.
  • The minimum age is 19 in British Columbia and Atlantic Canada, and 18 in Ontario, Quebec and WCLC's region.
  • You need an account in your own name. Registration checks your identity, age and, in most regions, where you live.

The national games are the same everywhere. What differs is who sells the ticket, the add-on (Extra in most of the country, Encore in Ontario) and how subscriptions and prizes are handled. Buying online doesn't change your odds; our odds calculator shows the real figures.

British Columbia: PlayNow

PlayNow.com is BCLC's gaming site and the only official place to buy B.C. lottery tickets online. BCLC describes it as "available exclusively to players located in BC." Its player agreement sets the eligibility rules: you must be at least 19, a resident of British Columbia, not enrolled in BCLC's Game Break self-exclusion program, and acting for yourself rather than on behalf of someone else. You also agree not to buy anything while you are physically outside B.C.

To open an account, PlayNow sends the details you enter to one of Canada's major credit bureaus to confirm your age and residency. PlayNow's help page notes that it does not check your credit history, only that the information matches.

Once registered, you can buy single tickets, buy for future draws, or set up a lottery subscription. PlayNow offers subscriptions for the next 2, 10, 52 or 104 draws, or "Never Miss a Draw", which keeps buying until you stop it. Subscriptions are paid from your account balance. If there isn't enough money in the account before a draw, the subscription is put on hold and no ticket is bought; if it stays on hold for a draw, it is stopped and you have to restart it. PlayNow's help page also notes that its automated subscription service doesn't run on Mondays, so funds for a Monday Daily Grand draw need to be in the account by 8 a.m. on Sunday.

BCLC's Lotto! app scans paper tickets to check them, and lets registered PlayNow players buy and manage tickets on their phone. At stores, BCLC's Advance Buy lets you buy up to 26 future draws of Lotto Max, Lotto 6/49, Daily Grand or BC/49.

Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba and the North: WCLC's region

The Western Canada Lottery Corporation runs lotteries for Alberta, Saskatchewan and Manitoba, with Yukon, the Northwest Territories and Nunavut as associate members. Online sales in this region come through three channels.

Lotto Spot app and Subscription Spot

WCLC sells single-draw tickets for Lotto 6/49, Western 649, Lotto Max, Western Max and Daily Grand through its Lotto Spot app, and the same games for consecutive draws, up to a year at a time, through a subscription. You must be inside WCLC's region to buy. Sask Lotteries' app page says purchases are paid with Visa, Mastercard or Apple Pay, that a mobile ticket covers one draw only, and that winning mobile tickets up to $1,000 must be validated by a lottery retailer to collect the prize. When we checked in early November 2026, that page also said Lotto Max was not available on the app "for a limited time", so check before you rely on it for a particular draw.

Alberta: PlayAlberta.ca

PlayAlberta, run by Alberta Gaming, Liquor and Cannabis, sells WCLC's lottery games including Lotto Max, Lotto 6/49, Western Max and Western 649. Its Lotto Max page lists Advance Play for 2 to 25 future draws, and subscriptions that buy your pre-set order for every draw until you cancel. PlayAlberta says a cart that includes a subscription must be paid by credit or debit card, and a recurring payment equal to the first purchase is charged each week until the subscription is cancelled, put on hold or a payment fails.

Manitoba: PlayNow.com

Manitoba uses its own version of PlayNow.com, run by Manitoba Liquor & Lotteries. You must be at least 18, a Manitoba resident and in Manitoba to buy. WCLC says PlayNow in Manitoba offers single tickets, Advance Play, subscriptions of up to 104 draws, and "Never Miss a Draw".

Age in WCLC's region

WCLC's policy is that people under 18 are refused the purchase or cashing of lottery tickets, and retailers may ask for proof of age from anyone who appears to be under 25.

Ontario: OLG.ca

OLG describes OLG.ca as Ontario's only website for lottery play. Ontario's gaming regulator, the AGCO, sets the age rule for gaming sites: players must be 19, except that 18-year-olds may use a site "solely for the purpose of purchasing a lottery ticket." The same AGCO standards require operators to check a player's location and to block attempts to get around it, for example with software that hides where a device really is.

OLG says players with an OLG.ca account can buy single tickets and subscriptions for Lotto Max, Lotto 6/49, Daily Grand, Ontario 49, Daily Keno, Pick-2, Pick-3, Pick-4 and Encore on the website, and single tickets on the OLG app. Its Direct Pay option accepts Visa, Mastercard, Visa Debit, Mastercard Debit and qualified prepaid cards issued by Canadian banks, so you don't have to load an e-wallet first. OLG's PlaySmart site points out that OLG doesn't charge to deposit funds, but your bank or card company may charge its own fees.

Quebec: lotoquebec.com

Loto-Québec sells its draw games online through an account on lotoquebec.com. Its FAQ lists the requirements: you must be 18 or older, a Québec resident and not enrolled in Loto-Québec's self-exclusion program. Registration asks for contact details, date of birth, address and occupation, and if necessary two pieces of identification. Loto-Québec says opening an account should take less than 10 minutes.

The online line-up includes Lotto Max, Québec Max, Lotto 6/49, Québec 49 and Extra, along with other Loto-Québec games. You deposit money into your account first and tickets are paid from the balance. Loto-Québec also offers a "Buy from a Retailer" option: you make your selections online, print a page with a bar code and take it to a store, with no account needed.

Atlantic Canada: alc.ca

Atlantic Lottery serves New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, Prince Edward Island and Newfoundland and Labrador. To open an alc.ca account you must be at least 19, live in Atlantic Canada and be physically in the region when you register and play. You may be asked for government ID to confirm your identity. Atlantic Lottery's Lotto Max page says you can buy online on alc.ca or the ALC Mobile App, for 1 to 10 advance draws, or set up a subscription that buys tickets automatically for every draw.

How the location and age checks work

Every corporation above sells only inside its own region, and they enforce it in two ways.

  • At registration, your name, address and date of birth are checked against an outside source. PlayNow in B.C. uses a credit bureau. Loto-Québec and Atlantic Lottery may ask for identification documents.
  • At purchase, the site or app checks where your device is. Ontario's regulator requires ongoing location checks "at reasonable intervals" and tools to detect software that hides your real location. PlayNow's player agreement makes buying from outside B.C. a breach of the agreement.

In practice this means a VPN or a friend's account isn't a workaround. Ontario's standards specifically target location-masking software, and PlayNow's agreement requires you to play only for yourself.

Sales cut-off times

Online and in-store sales for Lotto Max, Lotto 6/49 and Daily Grand close at the same moment nationwide: 10:30 p.m. Eastern Time on draw night. WCLC lists the local equivalents. Under standard time, which runs from the November clock change to the March one, they are:

Saskatchewan doesn't change its clocks, which is why its local cut-off shifts by an hour while Eastern Time moves around it. Atlantic Lottery holds its Lotto Max draws at 11:30 p.m. Atlantic Time. B.C. follows the same 10:30 p.m. Eastern close, which is 7:30 p.m. Pacific all year. A purchase that isn't finished before the cut-off is not entered in that night's draw.

Draw days are the same everywhere: Lotto Max on Tuesday and Friday, Lotto 6/49 on Wednesday and Saturday, and Daily Grand on Monday and Thursday.

OLG's PlaySmart site suggests one use for subscriptions: budgeting. If you set aside a fixed amount each month for lottery tickets, a subscription spends it at a steady pace instead of in a burst when a jackpot is in the news. A subscription also means you won't miss a draw because you forgot. The downside: it keeps buying until it ends or you stop it.

Online or paper: what changes

An online ticket is the same entry in the same draw as a paper ticket. The differences are practical:

  • No paper to lose. Online tickets are stored in your account, so a ticket can't be lost, washed or left unsigned. For a sense of how much money goes unclaimed on paper tickets each year, see our guide to unclaimed prizes and ticket expiry.
  • Automatic checking. Online tickets are checked for you. Sask Lotteries, for example, says mobile tickets in the Lotto Spot app are checked automatically.
  • Prize handling depends on the corporation. Read your lottery's rules for online wins. For example, WCLC requires winning mobile tickets up to $1,000 to be validated at a retailer. Large prizes still go through the corporation's normal claim process, with ID and, above set amounts, an interview. See claiming a prize, province by province.
  • Group play is harder online. An online ticket belongs to the account holder. If you buy for an office pool, keep a written record of who paid. Our lottery pools guide explains why that matters.

Unofficial ticket sellers and look-alike sites

Search for "buy Lotto Max online" and you'll find sites outside Canada that offer to buy tickets for you and send you a scan. WCLC's legal page is direct about this: other than its own online channels and the PlayNow and PlayAlberta sites, "no one else is authorized or endorsed by WCLC to sell lottery tickets on the Internet." The other corporations likewise describe their own sites as the official place to play in their regions.

The risks are practical. A third-party service is not bound by the lottery's rules, and the corporations pay prizes to the person entitled to the ticket under their own rules. A related risk is the "you've won" message. A real Canadian lottery doesn't contact you about a prize you didn't buy a ticket for, and doesn't ask for a fee to release a prize. Our guide to lottery scams in Canada covers the common versions.

To avoid look-alike sites, type the address yourself or use the corporation's official app from your phone's app store: playnow.com, playalberta.ca, olg.ca, lotoquebec.com or alc.ca, or WCLC's Lotto Spot app.

Frequently asked questions

Can I buy tickets for another province's lottery online?

No. Each corporation sells only to people physically located in its region, and most also require you to live there. Visiting another province doesn't let you buy from that province's site unless you meet its residency rules.

Can I buy Canadian lottery tickets from outside Canada?

Not from the official sites. All of them require you to be inside their region when you buy.

Are online tickets in the same draw as store tickets?

Yes. Online and paper tickets are entries in the same national draws, with the same numbers, odds and prizes.

Do online prizes get taxed?

No. Lottery prizes in Canada aren't taxed as income however the ticket was bought. Interest or investment income earned on the money later is taxable. See are lottery winnings taxed in Canada?
